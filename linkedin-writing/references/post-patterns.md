# Post patterns

Five shapes. Pick one, do not blend.

## A. Single post

**Target:** 900 to 1,800 characters.

```
Line 1     The object. A number, a filename, a quote from the product page.
Line 2     The consequence. What it meant in business terms.
Blank
Block 1    The mechanism. How it actually worked. One artefact.
Block 2    The thing that made it possible. Misconfiguration, trust, default.
Block 3    The part most people miss. The reason a scanner missed it.
Blank
Turn       What this means for the reader's job. One or two lines.
Close      A question answerable in one word, or an offer with no ask.
Blank
Tags       Three to five.
```

**Example of the turn:** "If your auth check lives in the frontend, the frontend is your security model. Move it."

## B. Series post

**Target:** 2,200 to 2,900 characters. LinkedIn's ceiling is 3,000.

```
DAY n: Claim

Hook       Two lines. Object first.
Bridge     Why the previous day left this open.
Blank
Section 1  The setup. What the system looked like.
Section 2  The attempt. First thing tried, and why it failed or half worked.
Section 3  The mechanism. The part that makes a reader say "oh".
Blank
The Fix    1. 2. 3. Real config, real command, real code.
Blank
Next       One line. What Day n+1 asks.
Tags       Three to five.
```

Rules:

- One section per post, not five. Five sections in 2,900 characters means each one is a headline.
- No box drawing characters. If you need a divider, use a capitalised line of plain text.
- Cross reference the previous part with a reason, not a reminder.
- Save "Follow so you don't miss" for part one and part last.

## C. Carousel

**Target:** 8 to 12 slides. Slide one is the only one that gets read for free.

Slide anatomy:

```
Slide 1    Hook. Six to nine words. One number or one contradiction.
Slide 2    The stakes. What was at risk, or what the product claimed.
Slide 3-9  One idea per slide. Headline of five words, then three lines maximum.
Slide 10   The fix. A list, still readable at thumbnail size.
Slide n    Close. One line, then the handle.
```

Rules:

- **Six words on a slide headline.** If it needs a comma, cut it.
- **No paragraph longer than three lines.** A carousel is not a document with page breaks.
- **One idea per slide.** If a slide has two, it is two slides and you are over budget.
- **Numbers on every slide that can carry one.** 141,000. 200 OK. 12,400 rows.
- **Slide 1 must work as the post's first line too.** Write it once, use it twice.
- **Last slide: one instruction.** Not three. Not a link tree.

Repurposing a ten part series into a carousel: cover, one slide per part, fix slide, close. For ten parts that is 13 slides, so merge the two weakest parts and land on 12. The carousel is a trailer. The article is the film.

## D. Comment

**Target:** 400 to 700 characters.

```
Line 1     Agree with the specific thing, in your own words. Not "Great post".
Block      The mechanism they left out, or the case that breaks their rule.
Block      What you would test, concretely.
Close      Optional. A question, or nothing.
```

Rules:

- Add, never correct. "One thing I would add" not "Actually".
- Name the artefact. If you cannot, the comment is not ready.
- No links. LinkedIn suppresses comments with links.
- Never pitch. The profile does the pitching.
- If the post is unresolved, answer the unresolved question. That is the highest value comment you can write.

## E. DM

**Target:** 600 to 1,300 characters. First message under 800 if the person has never replied.

```
Para 1     Who you are, in one clause. Only if they do not know you.
Para 2     The specific thing of theirs you are responding to.
Para 3     Your addition. Mechanism, not opinion.
Para 4     What you have not tested, or what you are unsure of.
Para 5     The concrete offer, with the ask detached.
Para 6     One question, answerable in one word.
```

Rules:

- **Never reuse a thread URL.** Open the conversation from the list and confirm the recipient before typing. A misdirected DM cannot be unsent.
- No attachments on the first message.
- No CV unless asked.
- The question at the end should be answerable with yes, no, or not yet. Anything longer and the reply costs them effort.
- If they go quiet, one follow up, and it must carry a new artefact. A repeat is nagging.

## Choosing between shapes

| Situation | Shape |
|---|---|
| One finding, one lesson | Single |
| A chain of findings with a sequence | Series |
| A process someone will save and reuse | Carousel |
| Someone posted something adjacent to your work | Comment |
| A specific person you want to work with | DM |

If you are unsure, write the single post first. Series and carousels are expansions of a single post that already works.
